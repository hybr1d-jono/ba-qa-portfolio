# Running a Team Retrospective

> **Portfolio sample.** How I facilitate retros in my scrum master role. Names and internal channels have been removed.

## Format

**Start → Stop → Continue.** Simple, quick to explain, and it pushes the team towards actions rather than complaints.

## Opening: the one-word check-in

Everyone describes how they're feeling in **one word**.

Typical answers: *Motivated. Sunny. Meh. Rushed. Hungry and confused. Annoyed.*

**Why I open with it:** it takes two minutes, gets every voice in the room early, and tells the facilitator a lot. Three people saying "rushed" or "annoyed" is a signal about workload before the retro has even started.

## Example outcomes (from a real retro, anonymised)

Every action has an **owner**. An action with no owner doesn't happen.

| Theme | Action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Faster code reviews** | Assign and notify a named reviewer on every merge request. Reviewer starts within a **1 hour timebox**. | Team |
| **Support escalations** | Rotate one developer each week to own the initial investigation on escalated tickets. A weekly chat bot message announces who's on. | Team |
| **AI learning** | Talk to the AI enablement lead about getting the team involved in upskilling. | Named team member |
| **Recognition** | Nominate a teammate for the monthly peer recognition points. | Team |

## What makes these good actions

- **Specific.** "1 hour timebox" beats "review faster".
- **Systemic.** The rotation and the bot fix the process, rather than relying on people remembering.
- **Owned.** Every row has a name or "Team" with a clear meaning.
- **Includes the positive.** Recognition keeps retros from becoming a complaints session.

## Follow-through

Open the next retro by reviewing the last one's actions: done, not done, or dropped and why. That's what makes the team trust the retro is worth their time.

## What this shows

Facilitation, turning discussion into owned and measurable actions, and reading team health. All of it carries straight into scrum master, BA and QA lead roles.
